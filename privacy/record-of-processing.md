**Start with the applicability record below.** GDPR Art. 30 requires a record of processing activities. Art. 30(5) lifts that one record-keeping duty for some organisations, on conditions. Decide whether it applies, write the decision down, and only then decide how much of the table you need. If you are unsure, keep the record.

## Art. 30(5) applicability record

**What the exemption is, and is not.** Art. 30(5) says the record-keeping duties in Art. 30(1) (controllers) and Art. 30(2) (processors) do not apply to an enterprise or organisation employing fewer than 250 persons, **unless** (any one is enough to lose the exemption):

1. the processing is likely to result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of data subjects;
2. the processing is not occasional; or
3. the processing includes special categories of data (Art. 9(1)) or personal data relating to criminal convictions and offences (Art. 10).

**It removes one record-keeping duty. It does not exempt you from the GDPR.** Lawful basis, transparency, security, rights requests, processor contracts, breach duties and the rest still apply whether or not you keep this record. Even where the exemption applies, the record is still the usual way to show how you comply, and Art. 49(1) (second subparagraph) and Art. 49(6) refer to documenting certain transfer assessments in "the records referred to in Article 30".

**How to read the result**

- **Exemption does not apply:** keep the full record below.
- **Exemption may apply:** it is not a conclusion that it does apply. Keep the decision above, review it on the date given, and expect to be asked for it.
- **Needs advice:** treat it as "does not apply" until you have advice.

## Notes on choosing a basis

- **Consent** must be freely given, specific, informed and as easy to withdraw as to give. If you rely on it, you need to store proof and honour withdrawal.
- **Legitimate interests** needs a short written balancing test: your interest, the necessity, and the impact on people.
- Do not pick a basis after the fact to fit what you already do. Choose it per purpose and say so in your privacy notice.
- If you act as a **processor** for a customer, the customer decides the basis. Your duties are in the processing agreement.
