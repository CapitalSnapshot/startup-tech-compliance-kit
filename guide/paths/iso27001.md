# Path to ISO/IEC 27001

> **Standing notice.** This is repository based, community maintained guidance. It is not legal advice, a formal compliance assessment, a certification or an independent security audit. Mappings and statements about frameworks are unverified unless their review label says otherwise ([review labels](../../docs/review-labels.md)).

ISO/IEC 27001 is a **standard for an information security management system (ISMS)**. A **certification body** audits you against it and issues a certificate. The standard is about the management system, not just technical controls.

**Review label: community draft.** Proposed guidance, not legal advice and not an assessment. **Get specialist advice** from your certification body or an adviser before you rely on it. The standard's text could not be read for this kit; identifiers and clause names here are unverified (see the [coverage report](../../docs/coverage/iso27001.md)).

> **Implementing an ISMS is not the same as being certified.** Certification is an audit result from a certification body (ideally an accredited one). Completing the kit, in whole or in part, is neither an ISMS nor certification, and nothing the kit prints means you are certified, audit-passed or "ready".

## What it is, in plain terms

- The core is clauses 4 to 10: context, leadership, planning (risk assessment and treatment), support, operation, performance evaluation (internal audit, management review) and improvement.
- **Annex A** lists controls (grouped as organisational, people, physical and technological). You select the ones that apply in a **Statement of Applicability** and justify any you exclude.
- Certification is normally for **three years**, with **yearly surveillance audits** and a recertification audit at the end.

## Implementing an ISMS, and being certified, are two different things

| | Implementing an ISMS | Certification |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Running a management system: context, leadership, risk assessment and treatment, controls, internal audit, management review, improvement | An independent certification body's audit of that system against the standard |
| Who decides | You | The certification body |
| Evidence of it | Your own records | A certificate, if the audit passes, with surveillance audits afterwards |
| The kit | Gives starting material for part of it | Does nothing toward it |

## Required work the kit does not do

**Clauses 4 to 10** (the management system itself: context, leadership, planning, support, operation, performance evaluation and improvement) and a **Statement of Applicability** are required work. The kit does not do them for you. Also required: a risk assessment and a risk treatment plan, an **internal audit** and a **management review**, each done by people. **A small control baseline is not a complete ISMS.**

## Prerequisites

1. A customer, partner or plan that needs it.
2. Top management who will own it. Clause 5 requires leadership commitment, a policy and assigned responsibilities.
3. A scope you can defend: which products, locations and teams.

## Steps

| # | Step | What you produce |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Context and scope.** Interested parties and their requirements; the boundary of the ISMS | A scope statement. Start from [`../../scoping/`](../../scoping/) |
| 2 | **Leadership.** Information security policy, roles and responsibilities | [`../../policies/information-security-policy.md`](../../policies/information-security-policy.md), [`../../policies/roles-and-responsibilities.md`](../../policies/roles-and-responsibilities.md) |
| 3 | **Risk method.** Define how you identify, score and accept risk | [`../../risk/methodology.md`](../../risk/methodology.md) |
| 4 | **Risk assessment.** Identify assets, threats and risks and score them | [`../../evidence/risk-register.md`](../../evidence/risk-register.md) |
| 5 | **Risk treatment plan and Statement of Applicability.** Choose treatments; select Annex A controls and justify exclusions | A treatment plan and a Statement of Applicability |
| 6 | **Operate the controls.** Technical, people, organisational | Controls 1 to 12 and their evidence |
| 7 | **Training and awareness**; communicate responsibilities | [`../../policies/security-awareness-training.md`](../../policies/security-awareness-training.md) and records |
| 8 | **Measure.** Decide what you monitor and how | Metrics and review notes |
| 9 | **Internal audit.** Audit your own ISMS, by someone independent of the thing audited | An internal audit programme and report |
| 10 | **Management review.** Top management reviews the ISMS and decides improvements | Minutes with decisions and actions |
| 11 | **Corrective action.** Record nonconformities and fix causes | A corrective action log |
| 12 | **Certification audit.** Stage 1 (documentation and readiness), then Stage 2 (implementation and effectiveness) | A certificate, if you pass |
| 13 | **Surveillance audits** yearly; recertify at three years | A continuing programme |

## Coverage matrix: what the kit covers

"Covers in part" means the kit gives starting material, never that the requirement is met. Every ISO mapping record is a community draft marked "needs expert review".

| Area | Kit | What the kit provides | What it does not |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clauses 4 to 10 (the management system) | **covers in part** | Policy and roles templates, a risk method and register, an annual management review in control 12, a scoping questionnaire | A functioning management system; leadership commitment; continual improvement |
| Risk assessment | **covers in part** | The risk methodology and register | An assessment method and results an auditor accepts |
| Risk treatment plan | **does not cover** | Nothing specific beyond treatment decisions in the register | A treatment plan tied to Annex A controls |
| Statement of Applicability | **does not cover** | Nothing | All of it |
| Annex A controls (technical and organisational) | **covers in part** | Controls 1 to 12 with evidence templates and verify commands | Selecting controls through the risk assessment; a check against the standard's identifiers |
| Internal audit | **does not cover** | Nothing (the kit's audit is an evidence freshness check, not an ISMS internal audit) | All of it |
| Management review | **covers in part** | Guidance and an annual review in control 12 | Minutes, decisions and actions for your system |
| Certification audit (Stage 1 and Stage 2) | **does not cover** | Nothing | All of it |
| Surveillance audits and recertification | **does not cover** | Nothing | All of it |

## Exit criteria: what completing each shows, and what it does not

| Exit criterion | What it shows | What it does not show |
|---|---|---|
| Scope and context recorded | The team has decided them | That the scope is acceptable to a certification body |
| All twelve controls have current, passing evidence on file (the audit says "internal evidence check complete") | The evidence is present, recent and recorded as passing | That any control works, or that an ISMS exists |
| Risk assessment, risk treatment plan and Statement of Applicability written by the team | The required documents exist | That they are adequate; that the controls named in them operate |
| Internal audit and management review carried out and recorded | The organisation examined itself | That an independent body agrees |
| Stage 1 and Stage 2 audits completed by a certification body | **Only here does certification become possible** | That surveillance audits will pass |

**"Internal checklist complete"** means the first two rows are true. It does not mean you have an ISMS, an audit result or a certificate.

## Common mistakes

- Treating ISO 27001 as a list of technical controls. Without risk treatment, internal audit and management review there is no ISMS.
- Excluding Annex A controls without a reason. Exclusions need justification.
- An internal audit by the person who built the thing.
- A scope so narrow it excludes what customers care about; the certificate then says little.
- Letting documents drift from practice. Auditors check both.
