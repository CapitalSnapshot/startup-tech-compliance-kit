## [0.1.0] first public release

First public release. It folds the work of the external review of 9 October 2026 into the original release. All compliance content is a community draft: no maintainer or specialist review has been recorded.

- **Claims audit (prompt 14, part A).** Every text file was searched for wording that could read as a compliance, certification or examination outcome, and each hit was read in context. Four real over-claims were reworded, all in the production-migration skill: "audit-ready controls" (skills README), "compliance-ready controls (SOC 2, ISO 27001, GDPR, DORA)" and "built to survive security due diligence" (SKILL.md), "evidence-ready for" (prompt A1) and "stand up to ... due diligence" (references README). The other hits were negations ("does not make you compliant"), the name of maturity level 4 ("Assured"), or tests. A **standing notice** (repository based, community maintained guidance; not legal advice, a formal compliance assessment, a certification or an independent security audit; mappings unverified unless the review label says otherwise) is now on the README and at the top of the framework guides, the screening pages, the applicability matrix, the privacy README and the generated mappings page. Seven privacy pages that had no review label now carry one. New `scripts/check_claims.py` fails CI on a prohibited phrase in any text file unless `scripts/claims_allowlist.json` lists the file and phrase with a reason, and fails on a stale allowlist entry. `scripts/prohibited_phrases.json` gained four prose-only phrases.

- **Specialist review scopes, record template and scope checker (roadmap item: specialist review, P2).** New `docs/specialist-reviews/`: a README stating that only material named in a published scope becomes specialist reviewed, a review record template (scope, date, reviewer role, exclusions, outcome), an update procedure, and four proposed scope files (GDPR and privacy, ISO/IEC 27001, SOC 2, DORA) that list every mapping record and document each covers and carry no reviewer and no date. New `scripts/review_scope.py` with `list`, `check` and `preview`; it never edits a file, and `check` rejects a proposed scope that claims a reviewer or date, a completed scope without a role, date, limitations or a decision for every item, a future date, and a reviewer role naming a maintainer or author. `CONTRIBUTING.md` gains "Offering a specialist review". No review label was changed: all 60 records remain community drafts. The internal briefs for reviewers are kept with the review working files and are not part of the public release.

- **NIS2 screening worksheet (review finding F-07).** The one-line NIS2 statement in the applicability matrix ("a covered sector and size class") and the questionnaire now point to a new worksheet, `scoping/extensions/nis2-screening.md`, in a new extensions area (`scoping/extensions/README.md`) that keeps regimes outside the core mappings apart and states that extensions are screening aids and the kit does not assess compliance with them. The worksheet records: sector, subsector and type of entity against Annexes I and II (and says the Directive does not list software vendors as a type, so unclear cases are "unsure"); size against Recommendation 2003/361/EC including partner and linked enterprises; the size-independent routes in Art. 2(2) to (4); the exceptions in Art. 2(5) and (7) to (10); jurisdiction and registration (Art. 26, 27, 3(3) and (4)); and, for every Member State involved, the national law, its status as the user found it, the date checked and the source. The kit states no Member State's transposition status. Outcomes are "potentially in scope", "not in scope based on a documented assessment" (needs every section complete, no "unsure", every Member State checked, a written rationale and a reviewer) and "requires advice" (the default). A section notes that an in-scope customer may ask for supply chain security terms by contract (Art. 21(2)(d), 21(3)), which is not a direct duty. Sources read: Directive (EU) 2022/2555 (Art. 1, 2, 3, 6 in part, 21(1) to (3), 26, 27, 41(1), Annexes I and II) and Recommendation 2003/361/EC (Annex Art. 2 and 3); both are on the source watch list. Not read: Commission and ENISA guidance, the Art. 21(5) implementing acts, any national law.

- **Art. 30(5) decision record and transfer assessment record (review findings F-05 and F-08).** `privacy/record-of-processing.md` now starts with an Art. 30(5) applicability record: persons employed (and how counted), whether processing is occasional, risk to rights and freedoms, special categories or criminal convictions data, a result ("exemption may apply", "exemption does not apply" or "needs advice"), rationale, decision owner, date and review date. It says the exemption lifts one record-keeping duty, not the GDPR, and lists the three conditions that remove it. The privacy README line that described the exemption was corrected (it omitted the 250-person condition and the criminal convictions condition). `privacy/international-transfers.md` now has a register that points to a twelve-part transfer assessment record: parties and roles (and whether the importer is subject to the GDPR), the route (adequacy decision, appropriate safeguards, derogation; module One to Four where standard clauses are used), the destination assessment following Clause 14 of Decision (EU) 2021/914, supplementary measures, onward transfers, sources, an approver other than the author, reassessment triggers and a reviewer test. New `privacy/international-transfers-example.md` is filled in with fictional parties and a fictional country. Sources read: GDPR Art. 30 and Chapter V (Art. 44 to 49) and Decision (EU) 2021/914 (Art. 1 to 4, Clauses 7, 8.7, 8.8, 9, 14); both added to the source watch list. The EDPB Recommendations 01/2020 are listed but not read (pending citation). Two mapping records were updated: the Art. 30 record no longer says the exemption has no decision template, and the Chapter V record moved from "needs expert review" (text not read) to "covered in part", with exclusions that say what the template does not decide.

- **Plain "covered" status (maintainer decision, 12 October 2026).** There are now six coverage statuses: covered, covered in part, not covered, not applicable, not assessed, needs expert review. "Covered" is allowed only on a record labelled maintainer reviewed or specialist reviewed; the validator rejects it on a community draft, so no record can say it yet and no generated page can read as a pass before a review has happened. It says how far the kit addresses a requirement, never that you meet it. Schema, validator, generated mappings page and coverage reports (counts and a "Covered" section), `docs/review-labels.md` and CONTRIBUTING updated; tests added for rejection on a community draft and acceptance on a reviewed record.

- **Assurance boundaries (review finding F-09).** The SOC 2 and ISO/IEC 27001 paths now say plainly that the kit cannot issue or guarantee a SOC 2 report or an ISO certificate. SOC 2 separates Type I (design at a point in time) from Type II (operation over a period) and lists the decisions only the team can make (system scope, criteria, evidence period). ISO separates implementing an ISMS from certification and says a small control baseline is not a complete ISMS. Each path has a coverage matrix using only "covers", "covers in part" and "does not cover", and exit criteria that state what each completed step shows and does not show. The maturity model says level 4 cannot be reached by completing the kit. The audit tool's final line is now "INTERNAL EVIDENCE CHECK COMPLETE" (was "ALL EVIDENCE PRESENT") with a sentence that it is not an examination, certification or audit. New `scripts/prohibited_phrases.json` and `audit/tests/test_status_wording.py` fail the build if any generated status output or any string in the tools implies an external outcome (for example "audit-ready", "SOC 2 ready", "certified", "compliant").

- **GDPR territorial scope (review finding F-01).** The matrix, roadmap, privacy README, control 08 and the questionnaire no longer say the GDPR applies because people are in the EU or EEA. New worksheet `scoping/gdpr-territorial-scope.md` (community draft) separates the establishment route (Art. 3(1)), offering to people in the Union (Art. 3(2)(a)), monitoring their behaviour (Art. 3(2)(b)) and the unclear case that needs advice, asks about an EU representative (Art. 27), and never concludes that you are or are not subject to the GDPR. Questionnaire section 4a added. The first-hour script prompts the check when personal data is involved.

- **DORA obligation types (review finding F-02).** The applicability matrix now has three DORA rows with an obligation type: direct statutory duty (a financial entity), contractual flow-down (terms a financial-entity customer may require, Art. 28 and 30) and oversight obligation (designated critical ICT providers, Art. 31). All 12 DORA mapping rows are labelled, and contract articles are never presented as direct duties. New page `scoping/dora-screening.md` (community draft) gives a screening path and a paraphrase of the Art. 30 contract elements. It notes that Art. 2(1)(u) lists ICT third-party service providers in the Regulation's scope and does not conclude that providers are outside it.

- **DPIA screening (review finding F-03).** `privacy/dpia-screening.md` is now a preliminary screening and says it is not a legal threshold. Serious indicators come only from GDPR Art. 35(3)(a) to (c) and the supervisory authority's published list (Art. 35(4)); any one of them, answered "yes" or "unsure", gives "DPIA likely required or seek advice" whatever the score. Further risk factors only move the outcome to "DPIA recommended" and are marked as needing maintainer review. "May not be required" needs a complete record, including the authority list consulted, and is never a conclusion. Added the Art. 35(7) minimum contents, Art. 35(2), (9), (11) and Art. 36(1) steps, a reviewable decision record, `scripts/dpia_screen.py` (the same rules, tested) and an example answers file.

- **Breach workflow roles (review finding F-06).** `runbooks/incident-response.md` now starts from your role for the affected personal data. A **controller** notifies the supervisory authority without undue delay and where feasible within 72 hours of awareness unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk, and assesses whether to tell the people affected (Art. 33(1), Art. 34). A **processor** notifies the controller without undue delay (Art. 33(2)); Article 33 gives the processor no 72-hour deadline. A customer contract may set a **contractual** deadline, kept separate from statutory ones. Added: an awareness timestamp and how it was decided, a table of each customer's contractual deadline, escalation contacts, a decision log, an Art. 34 assessment, and a note that other incident reporting regimes may apply. The 72-hour figure is now stated only in a controller context (runbook, privacy README, controls 08 and 09, incident report template). New `runbooks/tabletop-incident-notification.md` with three scenarios and expected answers (community draft).

- **Review labels and coverage statuses (review findings F-12 and F-14).** New `docs/review-labels.md` defines exactly three review labels (community draft, maintainer reviewed, specialist reviewed) with what each does not mean and who can grant it, and exactly five coverage statuses (covered in part, not covered, not applicable, not assessed, needs expert review). Blank values and reading silence as a status are not allowed; "not covered" and "not applicable" need a written rationale. CONTRIBUTING, the pull request template and the issue templates start proposals as community draft and say CI or maintainer approval does not imply legal or specialist approval. The README, the framework mappings and the applicability matrix now say that all compliance content is a community draft, that no coverage status is recorded yet, and that an item not listed has not been assessed. Corrected wording that said the authors had "reviewed" the mappings.

- **Mappings as data (review findings F-13 and F-10, structure).** The framework mappings now live in `controls/mappings.json`, validated against `controls/mapping.schema.json`, and `controls/framework-mappings.md` is generated from them. Each record has its own framework, edition, exact source reference, source URL, rationale, requirements covered, exclusions, dependencies, coverage status (one of five), limitations, change reference, and a review object (label, reviewer role, review date, decision, rationale, next review trigger). `scripts/validate_mappings.py` rejects missing metadata, invalid or blank statuses, a missing rationale for "not covered" or "not applicable", reviewer data on a community draft, missing reviewer data on a reviewed label, a future review date, an unknown control, a missing framework for a control, and a generated page that differs from a fresh build; CI runs it. The 48 existing mappings were moved over without inventing data: all are "not assessed" community drafts, and all are reported as incomplete where the old prose lacked a field. The per-row "Last reviewed" date and "Specialist-verified" column were removed because they implied a review.

- **Mapping backfill (review findings F-04 and F-10).** The 48 placeholder mapping records were filled in, framework by framework, and split where one control meets different requirements (60 records now). For the GDPR and DORA records, each reference was checked against the official text (Publications Office edition of the Official Journal) and the record states what is covered, what is excluded, what it depends on and its limits; DORA direct duties of a financial entity are kept apart from the Art. 30 terms a customer may require, and one term (return of a customer's data on termination) is recorded as "not covered". For SOC 2 and ISO/IEC 27001 the criteria and standard text could not be read, so every such record is "needs expert review" with its identifiers marked unverified and no standard text copied; ISO records separate Annex A controls from the management system clauses 4 to 10 and say the control baseline is not a complete ISMS. The generated page now shows the status counts per framework. All records remain community drafts with no reviewer recorded.

- **Generated coverage reports (review findings F-04 and F-13).** `docs/coverage/` now has one report per framework plus an index, generated from `controls/mappings.json` by `scripts/coverage_reports.py`. Each opens with the review label summary and the notice that the reports describe the state of the mappings, are not a measure of anyone's compliance, and have not been verified by a specialist unless the review label says so. They give counts by coverage status and review label, the edition mapped, and lists of the records that are "not assessed", "needs expert review", "not covered" (actual gaps), "not applicable" (documented exclusions) and incomplete. "Not assessed" is stated to be neither a gap nor a pass. There are no percentages, scores or readiness wording. The date shown is the new required `data_date` in `mappings.json`, so a fresh build is reproducible. CI fails if the committed reports differ from a fresh build.

- **Contact.** `MAINTENANCE.md` names one role mailbox (not a person) as the point of contact for source updates and offers to review the material. It is the only address in the repository; a test enforces that, and that the watch list names roles, not people.
- **Framework change process (review finding F-11).** New source watch list (`docs/maintenance/sources.json`, with a generated page) recording, for each source the kit relies on, the exact edition mapped, the official URL and how it was verified, what was actually checked and when (or that it has never been checked), an owner role and a review cadence. New `scripts/source_impact.py`: `list` shows every mapping record, control file and document affected by a source or edition change; `check` fails if the mappings use an edition the watch list does not name (or the reverse) or its page is stale; `release-notes` reports which sources are current, stale or never checked and how many mappings remain unreviewed. `MAINTENANCE.md` now defines four roles, nine steps from noticing a change to a reviewed release, and what to do if nobody can review in time. New issue template for framework, law or guidance updates. `VERSIONING.md` and `RELEASE.md` now require a release that changes compliance content to name the sources and editions affected and to list the mappings it leaves stale, unreviewed or incomplete. A worked example with a fictional edition change is in `docs/maintenance/worked-example.md`. CI runs the new check.

- Guide: the four layers (security, governance, legal, assurance), a four-level maturity model, a phased roadmap, and general paths to SOC 2 and ISO/IEC 27001.
- GDPR operating process: data inventory, record of processing with lawful basis, retention and deletion, data subject requests, international transfers, privacy by design review, DPIA and DPO screening, accountability.
- Risk methodology and a scored risk register; short policy templates and a vulnerability remediation standard.
- Environments and pipeline guidance; runbooks for incident response, restore test, cutover, rollback and rebuild.
- Evidence templates (evidence file, risk register, vendor register, access review, restore-test report, incident register and report, due diligence summary) and evidence-handling guidance.
- `audit/kit-audit.py`: an evidence completeness and freshness check (not a compliance assessment), with optional read-only GitHub rule checks.
- `verify/`: read-only commands per control for AWS (run against a real account), and Google Cloud and Azure (**untested**); provider template for contributions.
- The `production-migration` skill and prompt pack.
- Sanitise scan (identifiers, credentials, and files that must not be committed), 96 tests, CI on Python 3.9 to 3.13.
- Framework mappings drafted by the authors on 2026-10-09; **no row reviewed by a maintainer or a specialist**. The privacy and legal material is flagged throughout: **get specialist advice before relying on it**.
